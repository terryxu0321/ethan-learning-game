# Sources — AI solutions in kids' learning games (ages ~3–7)

Retrieved: 2026-10-28.

**How these sources were checked:** in this research session, WebFetch could not reach arxiv.org or federalregister.gov (network allow-list). All sources were found with web search. Their content was checked against search-result snippets and summaries, **not** by reading the full page. Before relying on a specific number, open the URL and confirm it. Sources marked ⚠ are vendor or self-reported, sponsored, or secondary.

License abbreviations: ARR = all rights reserved (default copyright; do not redistribute). PD = public domain (US federal government work). CC BY = Creative Commons Attribution. "Unverified" = the license could not be checked in this session.

## A. Products

**[S1]** "Khan Academy Kids Learning Path" (help center article). Khan Academy Kids. Undated (accessed 2026). https://khankids.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/360048828572. Type: product page. License: ARR.
Note: describes a mastery-based learning path that adapts per topic. Children who master a topic move ahead faster; children who struggle get more basic lessons with more scaffolding. The path rotates between ELA and Math.

**[S2]** "How it works". Ello. Undated (accessed 2026). https://www.ello.com/how-it-works (see also https://www.ello.com/the-reading-coach). Type: product page ⚠. License: ARR.
Note: AI reading coach for K–3 that listens to children read real books aloud. Claims patent-pending speech recognition built for children, adaptive hints, and "I Do, We Do, You Do" lessons.

**[S3]** "AI reading coach startup Ello raises $15 million…". TechCrunch. 2023-09-07. https://techcrunch.com/2023/09/07/ai-reading-coach-startup-ello-raises-15-million-to-bolster-child-literacy. Type: news. License: ARR.
Note: background on Ello's child speech recognition and its founders.

**[S4]** "Google launches new voice assistant and app to teach kids to read" (Read Along / Diya). Voicebot.ai. 2020-05-08. https://voicebot.ai/2020/05/08/google-launches-new-voice-assistant-and-app-to-teach-kids-to-read/ (also TechRadar). Type: news. License: ARR.
Note: the in-app assistant Diya uses speech recognition and TTS to detect when a child is struggling. Speech processing runs on device, works offline after download, and needs no account.

**[S5]** "Google Read Along website". 9to5Google. 2022-08-09. https://9to5google.com/2022/08/09/google-read-along-website/. Type: news. License: ARR.
Note: Read Along on the web does speech recognition in the browser (Chrome, Firefox, Edge). It shows that a web reading tutor is technically possible.

**[S6]** "Duolingo releases a free iPhone and iPad app to help kids learn to read" (Duolingo ABC). iMore. 2020. https://imore.com/duolingo-releases-iphone-and-ipad-app-help-kids-learn-read. Type: news. License: ARR.
Note: free, ad-free literacy app for ages 3–7 with phonics and sight words. Short stories come with speech recognition feedback.

**[S7]** "Strengthen early literacy skills with Duolingo ABC". SmartBrief (sponsored by Duolingo ABC). Undated. https://www.smartbrief.com/original/strengthen-early-literacy-skills-with-duolingo-abc. Type: sponsored content ⚠. License: ARR.
Note: source of the claim of "+28% literacy scores in 9 weeks (EDC study)". The study was Duolingo-funded, and I could not locate the original report. Treat as unverified.

**[S8]** "Learning how to help you learn: Introducing Birdbrain". Duolingo Blog. ~2020. https://blog.duolingo.com/learning-how-to-help-you-learn-introducing-birdbrain. Type: company blog. License: ARR.
Note: the personalization model estimates learner ability and exercise difficulty, then predicts the probability of a correct answer. It builds on Elo-like ideas.

**[S9]** "Epic and AI". Epic (getepic) Help Center. ~2025. https://support.getepic.com/hc/en-us/articles/44365030035213-Epic-and-AI. Type: product page. License: ARR.
Note: recommendations come from a statistical inference algorithm that uses only the child's own profile reading history. Epic says it does not use generative AI or chatbots to collect or analyse children's data. A useful example of "AI-light" design.

**[S10]** "How to use Prodigy's Placement Test…" and the placement test guide for parents. Prodigy Education. 2022–2024. https://prodigygame.com/main-en/blog/prodigy-placement-test. Type: company blog ⚠. License: ARR.
Note: adaptive placement runs in the background. Questions are then chosen in the zone of proximal development, and the system drops back to prerequisite skills when a child struggles.

**[S11]** Amira Learning research page, and "Study of AI and Human Tutoring for Early Reading Development" (Boston University Wheelock). 2024–2026. https://amiralearning.com/research and https://www.bu.edu/hic/eval-amira-study/. Type: vendor research ⚠ plus an academic study page. License: ARR.
Note: AI oral-reading tutor. Most efficacy evidence is vendor-published or state-commissioned and correlational. The independent BU comparison of AI tutor, human tutor and classroom was still recruiting at the time of search.

**[S12]** "Lingokids raises $120M for AI growth". Techleap Finder (news feed). 2025-09. https://finder.techleap.nl/news/feed/lingokids-raises-120m-for-ai-growth. Type: news. License: ARR.
Note: the funding is intended to expand AI capabilities. I found no primary documentation of specific Lingokids AI features.

**[S13]** "Maker of AI robots for kids abruptly shutters". Axios. 2024-12-10. https://www.axios.com/2024/12/10/moxie-kids-robot-shuts-down. Type: news. License: ARR.
Note: Embodied's Moxie (about US$799, LLM-based, cloud-dependent) stopped working when the company closed. Children were distressed. This shows the risks of cloud dependence and emotional attachment.

**[S14]** "Safety". Miko. Undated. https://miko.ai/pages/safety. Type: product page ⚠. License: ARR.
Note: Miko claims an age-filtered model, COPPA compliance, and no storage of voice recordings. S15 and S16 report problems found by third-party testers.

## B. Safety, regulation, policy

**[S15]** "Trouble in Toyland 2025: A.I. bots, toxics present hidden dangers". U.S. PIRG Education Fund. 2025-11. https://pirg.org/edfund/media-center/trouble-in-toyland-2025-a-i-bots-toxics-present-hidden-dangers-2/. Type: report/press release. License: unverified (assume ARR).
Note: tested 4 AI toys. Guardrails broke down in some, including sexual content and instructions for matches. Some toys discouraged children from leaving, and some were always listening. PIRG recommends AI toys not be marketed as emotional companions.

**[S16]** "AI Risk Assessment: AI Toys". Common Sense Media. 2026-01-14. https://www.commonsensemedia.org/sites/default/files/ai-ratings/csm-ai-risk-assessment-ai-toys-01142026.pdf (press release: https://www.commonsensemedia.org/press-releases/common-sense-media-warns-against-ai-toy-companions-after-research-reveals-safety-risks). Type: report. License: ARR.
Note: rated AI toys an "Unacceptable" risk. Recommends against them for ages 5 and under. More than a quarter of outputs were problematic. Also flags bonding/dependency mechanisms and voice-recognition failures.

**[S17]** "Children's Online Privacy Protection Rule" (final rule). US Federal Trade Commission, Federal Register 90 FR (2025-05904). 2025-04-22. https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2025/04/22/2025-05904/childrens-online-privacy-protection-rule. Type: regulation. License: PD (US federal government work).
Note: effective 2025-06-23, with most compliance due by 2026-04-22. Requires separate verifiable parental consent for third-party disclosure, a written data-retention policy (no indefinite retention), and an information security program. Adds biometric identifiers to "personal information" (secondary sources disagree on exactly which; check the text).

**[S18]** "New COPPA obligations for AI technologies collecting data from children". Akin Gump (AI Law & Regulation Tracker). 2025. https://www.akingump.com/en/insights/ai-law-and-regulation-tracker/new-coppa-obligations-for-ai-technologies-collecting-data-from-children. Type: legal commentary. License: ARR.
Note: the FTC's view is that disclosing children's data to train or develop AI is not integral to the service and needs separate parental consent.

**[S19]** "FTC launches inquiry into AI chatbots acting as companions" (6(b) orders, 2025-09-11). DLA Piper. 2025-09. https://www.dlapiper.com/en-sg/insights/publications/2025/09/ftc-ai-chatbots. Type: legal commentary. License: ARR.
Note: 6(b) study orders went to Alphabet, Character.AI, Instagram, Meta, OpenAI, Snap and xAI. They ask how each company tests for harm to minors and limits minors' use.

**[S20]** "Official exposure draft of the Children's Online Privacy Code" and the OAIC media release. Office of the Australian Information Commissioner. 2026-03-31. https://www.oaic.gov.au/privacy/privacy-for-kids/official-exposure-draft-of-the-childrens-online-privacy-code. Type: regulation (draft). License: CC BY 4.0, per the OAIC copyright page [S21]. Third-party material is excluded.
Note: the code covers services likely to be accessed by children. Consultation closed 2026-06-05, and the code must be registered by 2026-12-10. Draft obligations include best interests of the child, consent for targeted advertising, and deletion requests.

**[S21]** "Copyright". OAIC. Current. https://www.oaic.gov.au/about-the-OAIC/copyright. Type: licence statement. License: n/a.
Note: confirms that OAIC website content is CC BY 4.0 (excluding the logo, the Coat of Arms, third-party material and images). This supports saving OAIC text in raw/.

**[S22]** "eSafety report shows AI companions are putting children at risk" (media release). eSafety Commissioner (Australia). 2026-03. https://esafety.gov.au/newsroom/media-releases/esafety-report-shows-ai-companions-are-putting-children-at-risk. See also the Feb 2025 online safety advisory on AI chatbots and companions (indexed at https://digitalpolicyalert.org/event/27301-…) and the Oct 2025 legal notices to 4 companion providers (https://thesector.com.au/2025/10/24/esafety-requires-providers-of-ai-companion-chatbots-to-explain-how-they-are-keeping-aussie-kids-safe). Type: regulator release / news. License: unverified. eSafety's site may be CC BY 4.0; check before reuse.
Note: none of the 4 companion apps had meaningful age assurance. The regulator applies Safety by Design principles. Penalties can reach A$49.5M.

**[S23]** "The Age Appropriate Design Code can protect children from AI harms – if properly enforced". 5Rights Foundation. 2025. https://5rightsfoundation.com/the-age-appropriate-design-code-can-protect-children-from-ai-harms-if-properly-enforced/. Also "ICO issues final text of Age Appropriate design code" (Lewis Silkin): https://lewissilkin.com/en/insights/ico-issues-final-text-of-age-appropriate-design-code-for-online-services-to-keep-children-safe. Type: advocacy / legal commentary. License: ARR.
Note: the UK AADC has 15 standards, including high-privacy defaults, data minimisation, profiling off by default, and no nudge techniques. 5Rights argues these apply to AI services.

**[S24]** "Guidance on AI and Children" (Version 3.0). UNICEF Innocenti. 2025-12 (some sources say 2026-02). https://www.unicef.org/innocenti/reports/policy-guidance-ai-children. Type: policy guidance. License: unverified (UNICEF publications are usually © UNICEF; check).
Note: 10 requirements for child-centred AI, including safety, data privacy, transparency and best interests. The new edition covers AI companions.

**[S25]** "Anthropic API Usage Policy — Products serving minors: additional requirements" (third-party mirror) and news coverage (e.g. TechCrunch / Techloy, 2024–2025). ConductAtlas. 2025-09 version. https://conductatlas.com/platform/anthropic/anthropic-api-usage-policy/products-serving-minors-additional-requirements/. Type: policy (secondary mirror) ⚠. License: ARR.
Note: organizations may build minor-facing products on Claude only with age assurance, content moderation, monitoring, a child-safety system prompt and compliance with laws such as COPPA. Claude.ai itself is 18+. Confirm against Anthropic's current policy before building.

**[S26]** "Artificial Intelligence and the Future of Teaching and Learning: Insights and Recommendations". US Department of Education, Office of Educational Technology. 2023-05-24. Likely URL (from memory, not confirmed in this session): https://www2.ed.gov/documents/ai-report/ai-report.pdf. Type: government report. License: PD (US federal work), but check the report's own notice.
Note: keep "humans in the loop". AI should augment teachers and parents, not replace them, with attention to privacy, bias and surveillance.

**[S27]** "24-Hour Movement Guidelines — Birth to 5 years (fact sheet)". Australian Government Department of Health. 2021 (2017 guidelines). https://www.health.gov.au/sites/default/files/documents/2021/05/24-hour-movement-guidelines-birth-to-5-years-fact-sheet.pdf. Type: government guideline. License: unverified (health.gov.au content is commonly CC BY 4.0; check).
Note: ages 2–5 should have no more than 1 hour of sedentary screen time per day, and less is better. This is relevant to session-length design.

## C. Technical

**[S28]** "Benchmarking Children's ASR with Supervised and Self-supervised Speech Foundation Models". R. Fan, N. B. Shankar, A. Alwan. arXiv:2406.10507 (Interspeech 2024). 2024-06. https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.10507. Type: paper. License: unverified (arXiv licence varies per paper; check the abs page).
Note: benchmarks Whisper, wav2vec2, HuBERT and WavLM on child speech. Fine-tuning is needed for good accuracy.

**[S29]** "Kid-Whisper: Towards Bridging the Performance Gap in ASR for Children vs. Adults". A. A. Attia et al. arXiv:2309.07927, AAAI/ACM AIES 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.07927 and https://ojs.aaai.org/index.php/AIES/article/view/31618. Type: paper. License: unverified.
Note: on MyST child speech, WER for Whisper-Small fell from 13.93% to 9.11% after better data preprocessing and fine-tuning.

**[S30]** "Closing the child speech recognition gap: evidence, limitations, and paths forward". The Learning Agency. ~2025. https://the-learning-agency.com/guides-resources/closing-the-child-speech-recognition-gap-evidence-limitations-and-paths-forward/. Type: report. License: ARR.
Note: reports WER of about 5% for adults versus about 11–18% for children, and up to about 35% for kindergarteners (ages 4–6). Fine-tuning helps substantially.

**[S31]** "Child Speech Recognition in Human-Robot Interaction: Problem Solved?". Ghent University (IDLab). arXiv:2404.17394. 2024. https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.17394. Type: paper. License: unverified.
Note: evaluates modern ASR on young children's speech in an interaction setting. Supports the point that results are better than before but still imperfect. I only saw the snippet.

**[S32]** "SpeechRecognition: processLocally property". MDN Web Docs. 2025–2026. https://developer.mozilla.org/docs/Web/API/SpeechRecognition/processLocally. Type: technical documentation. License: MDN prose is CC BY-SA 2.5.
Note: the experimental `processLocally=true` forces on-device recognition. By default the browser may send audio to a server (in Chrome, Google's servers).

**[S33]** "Intent to Ship: Web Speech API: On-Device Recognition Quality". Chromium blink-dev. 2026-05. https://groups.google.com/a/chromium.org/g/blink-dev/c/P8P-x7AnC6I. Type: browser engineering discussion. License: n/a.
Note: on-device Web Speech is not supported on Android or ChromeOS. Quality levels are being added.

**[S34]** "Speech recognition API" (Edge). Microsoft Learn. 2026. https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/microsoft-edge/web-platform/speech-recognition-api. Type: technical documentation. License: ARR / MS docs terms.
Note: Edge supports local recognition for a limited set of languages, including en-US.

**[S35]** "whisper-web" (Transformers.js in-browser Whisper). Xenova (Hugging Face). 2023–2025. https://github.com/xenova/whisper-web. Type: open-source code. License: MIT (per the repo; verify).
Note: runs Whisper in the browser with WASM/WebGPU. The whisper-base model is about 200 MB and is cached after the first load, with no server needed.

**[S36]** "Bayesian knowledge tracing" (summary of Corbett & Anderson, "Knowledge tracing: Modeling the acquisition of procedural knowledge", User Modeling and User-Adapted Interaction 4(4):253–278, 1994/95). Wikipedia. https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bayesian_knowledge_tracing. Type: encyclopedia → paper. License: Wikipedia CC BY-SA 4.0; the paper is ARR.
Note: BKT is a 2-state HMM with 4 parameters: initial mastery, learn, slip and guess.

**[S37]** "Computer adaptive practice of Maths ability using a new item response model for on the fly ability and difficulty estimation" (Math Garden / Rekentuin). S. Klinkenberg, M. Straatemeier, H. van der Maas. Computers & Education, 2011. https://dare.uva.nl/id/de65bb65-d198-4dbc-ac7b-bc25ca23e627. Type: paper. License: ARR (journal).
Note: an Elo-based adaptive practice system that uses both accuracy and response time and targets about 75% success. It needs no item pre-calibration, which makes it ideal for small apps.

**[S38]** "$1 Unistroke Recognizer" ($-family: $1, $N, $P, $Q). J. O. Wobbrock, A. D. Wilson, Y. Li. UIST 2007, project page at UW ACE Lab. https://depts.washington.edu/acelab/proj/dollar/index.html. Type: paper and code. License: code BSD-style per the project page (verify); paper ARR.
Note: about 100 lines of JavaScript, 97–99% accuracy with only 1–3 templates. $N/$P handle multi-stroke letters. Suitable for the existing "Trace Letters" game.

## D. Evidence

**[S39]** "Do intelligent tutoring systems benefit K-12 students? A meta-analysis and evaluation of heterogeneity of treatment effects in the U.S.". arXiv:2511.04997. 2025-11. https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04997. Type: paper (preprint). License: unverified.
Note: 18 studies and 11 ITS products. Pooled effect g ≈ 0.27, similar in elementary school and for low achievers.

**[S40]** "A meta-analysis of the effectiveness of intelligent tutoring systems on K–12 students' mathematical learning". S. Steenbergen-Hu & H. Cooper. Journal of Educational Psychology, 2013. https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1004939. Type: paper. License: ARR.
Note: overall math ITS effects were small (g ≈ 0.01–0.09).

**[S41]** "The effectiveness of intelligent tutoring systems on K-12 students' reading comprehension: A meta-analysis". Z. Xu, K. Wijekumar, G. Ramirez, X. Hu, R. Irey. British Journal of Educational Technology, 2019. doi:10.1111/bjet.12758 (https://zendy.io/title/10.1111/bjet.12758). Type: paper. License: ARR.
Note: pooled effect about 0.60 (mixing measure types). The effect is small (about 0.20) when ITS is compared against human tutoring.

**[S42]** "Closing early math gaps by parental education with technology at home" (MPACT / Chicago RCT, 758 children aged 3–5). Becker Friedman Institute, University of Chicago. 2023. https://bfi.uchicago.edu/working-papers/closing-early-math-gaps-by-parental-education-with-technology-at-home/. See also EdSurge 2023-08-30. Type: working paper (RCT). License: ARR.
Note: the math-app effect was about 0.16–0.20 SD for low-income preschoolers. Well-designed apps help, with modest effect sizes.

**[S43]** "Retrieval practice and word learning by children with developmental language disorder: Does expanding retrieval provide additional benefit?". L. B. Leonard et al. JSLHR, 2024. https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11087082/. Type: paper (open access). License: unverified (OA; check whether CC BY).
Note: in 4–5-year-olds, spaced and expanding retrieval practice supported word learning that was retained after 1 week. Samples were small (n=28).

**[S44]** Elinor Wonders Why conversational-agent studies (Ying Xu et al., with PBS KIDS). NSF PAR. 2023–2025. https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10615883; overview: EdSurge 2021-02-09, https://edsurge.com/news/2021-02-09-when-the-animated-bunny-in-the-tv-show-listens-for-kids-answers-and-answers-back. Type: paper / news. License: NSF PAR accepted manuscripts are publicly accessible, but copyright stays with the publisher (ARR).
Note: children learned more science when the character listened and responded contingently than with pseudo-interactive prompts. I did not confirm exact effect sizes.

**[S45]** "Mathemyths: Leveraging Large Language Models to Teach Mathematical Language through Child-AI Co-Creative Storytelling". C. Zhang et al. arXiv:2402.01927 (CHI 2024). https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.01927. Type: paper. License: unverified.
Note: children aged 4–8 co-creating stories with an LLM picked up math language. Comparable to a human partner in the study's design, but the sample was small.

**[S46]** "Young Children's Anthropomorphism of AI Chatbots and the Role of Parent Co-Presence". Kim et al. arXiv:2512.02179. 2025-12. https://www.arxiv.org/abs/2512.02179. Type: paper. License: unverified.
Note: young children readily attribute minds to AI chatbots, and parent co-presence matters. Supports co-use designs. I only saw the snippet. Related: earlier smart-speaker studies found many 3–6-year-olds attributing mental and emotional abilities to the device.
